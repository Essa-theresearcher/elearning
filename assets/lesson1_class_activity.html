Class Activity: How Computers Actually Work

This is the hands-on portion you'd normally do live in class, adapted so you can work through it on your own. Take your time — nothing here is timed or graded.

Six short activities · ~25–30 minutes total · A notebook or the notes app on your phone is useful here

1Binary Detective

Type any single letter. Guess its encoding number before you reveal it.

2Bytecode Explorer

Here's what a simple function compiles to. Study it, then answer below.

def add(a, b): return a + b >>> import dis >>> dis.dis(add) 2 0 LOAD_FAST 0 (a) 2 LOAD_FAST 1 (b) 4 BINARY_ADD 6 RETURN_VALUE
BINARY_ADD LOAD_FAST twice just fetches a and b onto the stack. BINARY_ADD is the one instruction that does the actual math — everything else is bookkeeping.

3Reference or Copy?

Predict the output before revealing it.

a = [1, 2, 3] b = a b.append(4) print(a)
[1, 2, 3, 4] b was never a separate copy — it's another name pointing at the same list as a. Changing it through b changes what a sees too.

4Predict, Then Check

Same idea, different type. Does this behave the same way?

x = 10 y = x y = y + 5 print(x, y)
10 15 Numbers are immutable. y = y + 5 doesn't change the object 10 — it makes y point to a brand-new object, 15, while x still points to the original 10. Lists can be changed in place; numbers can't.

5Field Notes: Spot the Processes

A real-world task using your own machine — no reveal, just observation.

Open Task Manager (Windows) or Activity Monitor (macOS), and look through the list while nothing unusual is running.
